Muon neutrinos from the atmosphere oscillate into tau neutrinos ($\nu_\tau$) on their way through the Earth. At low energies, though, $\nu_\tau$ have too little energy to make tau leptons, so nobody has been able to see them appear.

With John Beacom, Nicole Bell and Matthew Dolan, we propose a practical way to do it in JUNO, a large liquid-scintillator detector in China. Neutrinos of any flavor can knock neutrons out of carbon nuclei. The neutron's scattering and its later capture give a clean two-part signal, seen before in KamLAND but only as a background. If muon neutrinos oscillate into $\nu_\tau$, JUNO should see 55 such events per year. If they simply disappeared instead, it would see only 41.

With better laboratory measurements of neutrino-carbon interactions, JUNO can reach $5\sigma$ sensitivity to $\nu_\tau$ appearance within five years.
