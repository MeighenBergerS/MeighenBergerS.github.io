title: The glowing life of the deep Pacific
paper: Li:2026jgr
---
Ocean neutrino telescopes look for faint flashes of light deep in the sea. They are not alone down there. Many deep-sea organisms make their own light, and for a neutrino telescope that light is background.

In this study, led by my PhD student Ruohan Li, we analyze four years of data from the STRAW instruments, pathfinders for the P-ONE telescope, at 2 to 2.6 km depth off the coast of British Columbia. More than 1.3 million camera images and continuous light-sensor recordings show which colors the organisms emit and when. The flashes follow the tides, which tells us they are mostly triggered when currents push organisms against the instruments. We estimate about 0.03 light-emitting organisms per cubic meter.

The same data serve two communities. Marine biologists learn about life in the deep Pacific, and neutrino physicists learn how to model their background.
