Whether neutrinos and antineutrinos behave differently, known as CP violation, is one of the main goals of the next generation of neutrino experiments. The usual approach sends a neutrino beam from an accelerator to a distant detector.

With my PhD student Ho Man Yim and with John Beacom, Nicole Bell and Matthew Dolan, we show that low-energy neutrinos made by cosmic rays in the atmosphere can do the same job. We compare neutrinos arriving from above with those coming up through the Earth, a ratio that cancels many uncertainties. For Hyper-Kamiokande, the first detector with enough of these events, this approach can surpass the sensitivity of the accelerator experiment T2HK where CP violation is largest.

Getting there needs additional, but realistic, work on theoretical uncertainties. If it succeeds, atmospheric neutrinos provide an independent check on billion-dollar beam experiments.
