The Sun makes electron neutrinos, but many change flavor before they reach Earth. Measuring both the electron neutrinos and the total of all flavors was how the SNO experiment proved that neutrinos oscillate.

With Jayden Newstead, John Beacom, Nicole Bell and Matthew Dolan, we show that the DUNE detector can do the same with argon. Solar neutrinos of any flavor can excite argon nuclei through neutral-current interactions, and the nucleus then emits a characteristic gamma ray. Combined with DUNE's other solar channels, this would give the most precise measurements of the solar mixing parameters using solar neutrinos. Comparing them with reactor measurements in JUNO would be a sharp test for new physics.

Getting there requires dedicated, but realistic, work on DUNE's low-energy capabilities and on neutrino-argon cross sections.
