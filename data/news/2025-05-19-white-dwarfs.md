White dwarfs are the dense, cooling remnants of stars like the Sun. Thanks to the Gaia mission, we now know many of them within a few dozen light-years of Earth, with well-measured properties.

With Sandra Robles and colleagues, we ask whether these stars can capture dark matter. We select ten cold, isolated white dwarfs within 13 parsecs and model their interiors in detail. Captured dark matter could annihilate into long-lived particles that escape the star and decay into gamma rays. Current Fermi-LAT data show no excess. Future gamma-ray telescopes such as CTA, LHAASO and SWGO could probe dark matter-nucleon interactions down to about $10^{-41}$ cm$^2$ for dark matter heavier than a TeV, competitive with the LZ direct-detection experiment.

Stars in our neighborhood thus complement the large detectors deep underground.
