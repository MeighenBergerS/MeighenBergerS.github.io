Dark matter particles passing through the Sun can be captured and annihilate into neutrinos. For light dark matter, below a few GeV, this fails. The Sun's core is so hot that the particles evaporate before they annihilate.

With Sandra Robles, we show that Jupiter does better. Its core is much cooler, and its gravity is still strong enough to hold on to light dark matter. Neutrinos from dark matter annihilating inside Jupiter could then be seen in water Cherenkov detectors. We find that Hyper-Kamiokande could reach interaction strengths of $2\times10^{-35}$ cm$^2$ for dark matter lighter than 2 GeV. That surpasses current limits from the Sun and from direct detection in this mass range.

Super-Kamiokande, ORCA and the IceCube Upgrade can test the same idea, so several running and upcoming detectors can make this search.
