Neutrino telescopes in ice and water record faint patterns of light, and turning those patterns into a neutrino's energy and direction is a hard inverse problem. Machine learning now does this well inside individual experiments. Comparing methods across experiments has been nearly impossible, because the data are not public.

With colleagues, we built NuBench, an open benchmark. Using the Prometheus simulation, we generated almost 130 million neutrino interactions between 10 GeV and 100 TeV in six detector layouts inspired by existing and proposed telescopes. We then test four reconstruction algorithms, including those used in KM3NeT and IceCube, on tasks such as energy and direction reconstruction. My part was the simulation code and the public data sets, which we first presented at ICRC 2025.

Anyone can now train and compare a new method on the same footing, and detector designers can see how layout choices affect reconstruction.
