Cosmic rays hit the atmosphere constantly and produce showers of particles, including neutrinos. Satellites and balloons measure the cosmic rays directly, but only at specific places and times, and their results do not fully agree.

With Jayden Newstead and Louis Strigari, we show that large neutrino detectors such as Hyper-Kamiokande can measure the cosmic rays through the atmospheric neutrinos they produce. These detectors see the whole sky and run for decades. We find they can distinguish between competing cosmic-ray models with high significance, even with today's uncertainties in neutrino interactions. Our reconstruction technique reduces the uncertainty on the neutrino flux from about 20% to about 7%. That in turn improves Hyper-K's sensitivity to the mixing angle $\theta_{23}$ by a factor of 2.

Neutrino detectors usually rely on cosmic-ray models. Here they become instruments for measuring them.
