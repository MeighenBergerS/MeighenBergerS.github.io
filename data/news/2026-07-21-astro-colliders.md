Cosmic accelerators reach collision energies far beyond anything we can build on Earth. When protons there hit gas or light, they make neutrinos, and how many depends on how likely those collisions are. That probability, the cross section, is measured in the lab only up to the energies of the LHC.

With Carlos Argüelles and colleagues, we turn this around. We pin down every astrophysical ingredient of a neutrino source from light observations or theory, so that the cross section is the only unknown left. Applied to the sources IceCube has linked to neutrinos, such as NGC 1068 and TXS 0506+056, and to the record-energy KM3NeT event, this bounds proton collisions at center-of-mass energies up to about $10^5$ GeV. Some of these lie well beyond the reach of the LHC. My part was the calculation of how $pp$ and $p\gamma$ collisions produce neutrinos.

As neutrino telescopes find more sources, each one becomes a new collider experiment.
