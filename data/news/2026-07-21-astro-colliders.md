Cosmic accelerators reach collision energies far beyond any machine on Earth, and the neutrinos they emit carry information about those collisions. With Carlos Argüelles and colleagues, we fix every astrophysical ingredient of a source from light observations, so that the proton collision cross section is the only unknown left. Applied to IceCube's neutrino sources and the record-energy KM3NeT event, this bounds collisions at energies up to about $10^5$ GeV, some well beyond the LHC. My part was computing how $pp$ and $p\gamma$ collisions produce neutrinos.
