The highest-energy cosmic rays may be accelerated near supermassive black holes, where dark matter can also pile up into dense spikes. With Bhupal Dev and Matheus Hostert, we point out that if dark matter scattered too easily off nuclei, it would break them apart before they reach the energies we observe. If these cosmic rays come from sources like NGC 1068, this rules out new territory for dark matter between 3 and 30 MeV. How cosmic rays are accelerated is still uncertain, so these are not yet robust limits, but they connect two open questions in a new way.
