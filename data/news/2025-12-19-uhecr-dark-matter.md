The highest-energy particles ever seen are cosmic rays, accelerated far outside our Galaxy, possibly near supermassive black holes. Dark matter may pile up near these black holes into dense spikes.

With Bhupal Dev and Matheus Hostert, we point out that the two have to coexist. If dark matter scattered too easily off protons and nuclei, it would break heavy nuclei apart and stop them from reaching the energies we observe at Earth. If the highest-energy cosmic rays come from sources like the galaxy NGC 1068, this excludes interactions stronger than about $10^{-33}$ cm$^2$ for a 1 GeV dark matter particle, and rules out new territory for masses between 3 and 30 MeV.

How cosmic rays are accelerated is still uncertain, so these are not yet robust limits. They connect two open questions in a new way, complementary to direct searches on Earth.
